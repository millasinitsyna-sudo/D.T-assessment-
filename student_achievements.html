Pathway to University

Studying Chinese opens doors to university courses and careers in international business, diplomacy, and translation. As the second most spoken language globally, Chinese gives students an advantage when traveling overseas, offering job and educational opportunities beyond New Zealand. Furthermore, learning a language boosts cognitive thinking, problem-solving, and concentration, while deepening your understanding of foreign cultures. Many university courses recommend language studies, and proficiency in Chinese can open up scholarship opportunities both locally and abroad. Nother perk is that by adding Chinese to your resume it helps you stand out from thousands of other applicants—offering. Overall learning Chinese gives benefits for getting into university and beyond.

Chinese Learning Stars

Hear from some of our current students about their personal learning journeys and tips!

Shayda - year 11 CN100

Personal learning journey of Chinese in our class: "I always really liked learning new languages. I started taking Asian languages in year 9 where they taught us chinese and japanese. I decided to continue taking Chinese in year 10 and 11 and my pronunciation and vocabulary has improved a lot in those two years. While learning Chinese I made new friends who were very friendly and helped me a lot with my learning. Therefore learning Chinese has been a very worthwhile experience for me."

Tips you would like to share: "My tip would be to learn at least fifteen words a day and in a few weeks you will have learned quite a bit of vocabulary. Being consistent with your learning helps with remembering and obtaining more knowledge.

Christina - year 11 CN100

Personal learning journey of Chinese in our class: "So when I was younger I was very fluent at Chinese, but as life progressed my mother stopped speaking it to me and I lost the language. Wellington East Girls' College Chinese language class has helped me grow and regain words that I have forgotten, boosting my confidence in speaking Chinese. In the future I plan to continue learning Chinese at WEGC to expand my vocabulary and learn more about the culture."

Tips you would like to share: "A tip that I would like to share is to nail pinyin and tones down early as it will make it so much easier to pronounce words correctly in the future."
